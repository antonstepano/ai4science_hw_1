          A multi-force microscale model for lunar dust detachment and
                       dispersion induced by rover mobility
                 Yanrong YE, Pengcheng ZHAO, Ruotong WANG, Zhiyong SHU & Hua QIAN*
                                        School of Energy and Environment, Southeast University, Nanjing 211189, China

                                   Received August 29, 2025; accepted February 5, 2026; published online August 27, 2026



       Abstract Lunar rover mobility inevitably induces dust lifting, posing risks to onboard instruments. This study develops a
       physics-based model that incorporates van der Waals forces, electrostatic interactions, and Coulomb forces to analyze the
       detachment and dispersion behavior of lunar dust. Numerical simulations using MATLAB are conducted to determine the
       critical detachment radius under varying driving speeds and slip rates. Results indicate that increasing either speed or slip rate
       reduces the detachment threshold from 19.11 to 17.79 μm. Further analysis of the simulated dust-dispersion images reveals a
       clear stratification in ejection height as a function of particle size. Under high-velocity and high-slip-rate conditions, small
       particles (radius < 20 μm) dominate the upper portion of the dust plume, reaching vertical propagation heights of up to 1.8 m,
       which substantially exceed the height of the rover itself. Validation against Apollo mission imagery confirms the model’s
       applicability. These findings provide theoretical guidance for dust mitigation strategies in future lunar rover missions.
       Keywords lunar dust, fender, detachment, smooth rotating wheel, theoretical model
